Customer Data Handling

This policy states how support staff may access, share, and retain customer data while resolving a ticket. It applies to every member of the support organisation, including contractors and temporary staff, from the first day of access. Breach of this policy is treated as a disciplinary matter regardless of whether harm resulted.

Access on a Need Basis

An agent opens a customer record only when an open ticket requires it. Curiosity is not a business need, and neither is checking an account belonging to a colleague, a family member, or a public figure. Every record access is logged against the agent's identity and sampled monthly.

Where a ticket requires data from an account the agent is not assigned to, the agent requests access through the duty manager rather than using a shared credential. Shared credentials are prohibited outright, because an action taken under one cannot be attributed to a person.

Sharing and Transmission

Customer data leaves the platform only through the approved ticketing system or the corporate secure transfer service. An agent never sends customer data to a personal email address, never pastes it into a public or third-party chat service, and never uploads it to an external file-sharing site — including one the customer themselves suggested.

Where a customer asks for their own data, the agent verifies identity using two independent factors from the account record before sending anything. A request that arrives from an address not on the account is refused, and the refusal is recorded.

Retention on the Desktop

An agent may hold an extract of customer data on a managed device for as long as the ticket is open, and deletes it when the ticket closes. Extracts are never stored on removable media and never retained after the ticket closes "in case it is needed again". The ticket itself is the record of the work; a local copy is a liability with no owner.

Reporting a Suspected Breach

An agent who suspects data has been disclosed reports it to the duty manager within one hour, whether or not the disclosure is confirmed and whether or not the agent caused it. Late reporting of a breach is treated more seriously than causing one, because the response window is what limits the harm.

Reviewed annually by the Data Protection Officer. Version 4.1.
